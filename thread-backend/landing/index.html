Where forgetting costs you something.

Anywhere the relationship matters more than the meeting.

Raising a round
Forty investor meetings in a quarter. Know which partner pushed on churn, who asked for the deck twice, and who you still owe an answer.
Client meetings
Walk into the review already knowing what they raised last quarter and what you promised to do about it.
Networking events
A dozen introductions an hour. Keep every name, and the reason each one was worth having.
Renegotiations
The last conversation set the terms. See what was agreed and what was left open before you sit down.
Recruiting and interviews
Fifty candidates in a week. Greet each one by name, and pick up where the last conversation stopped.
Conferences and reunions
The people you see once a year, whose names arrive four seconds too late. Thread gets there first.

It gets better every time you meet someone.

Thread keeps a map of the people you have met and what you talked about. Every conversation adds to it, so the second time you meet someone it already knows about the first.

A memory you can trust in the room.

  • It never guesses a name

    If Thread isn't sure who someone is, it adds them as a new contact instead of guessing. If there is no face in the photo, it says so.

  • It never guesses pronouns

    Thread uses a person's name, or they. It never assumes anything from a face.

  • Nobody is added in secret

    A new face is saved only during a capture you started. It appears in your list straight away, ready to be named or forgotten.

  • It runs on your computer

    Faces are matched on a backend you run yourself. No photo goes to an outside face service. Only the text of what was said goes to a language model, to pull out names and follow-ups.

  • Forget means forget

    Forgetting someone deletes their face, their notes and every meeting.

For everyone in the room. And for people this happens to every day.

At a conference you meet a dozen people in an hour and forget most of them by the evening. Thread gives you those names back.

For people living with face-blindness, hearing loss or early memory loss, that isn't an occasional embarrassment. It is every introduction, every day. Same product, much higher stakes.
